In 1835, Ohio and Michigan went to war.
Over this: a thin strip of land called Toledo.
Two surveys had drawn the border in two different places.
Ohio said: it's ours. Michigan said: no, it's ours.
Michigan's governor was twenty-four years old. He raised a thousand men.
Ohio raised six hundred.
At the Battle of Phillips Corners, Michigan fired its muskets... into the air.
Nobody was hit.
The war's only injury: a deputy sheriff, stabbed with a penknife, by a man named Two Stickney. He survived.
In the end, Congress gave Toledo to Ohio, and gave Michigan most of a cold, forested peninsula up north.
Michigan said no. Then, in a vote so cold it's called the Frostbitten Convention, Michigan said yes.
A few years later, they found copper up there. And iron. Lots of it.
Michigan lost the war... and won the peninsula.
